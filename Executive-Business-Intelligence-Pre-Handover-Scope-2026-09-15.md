# Per-Module Executive/Business Intelligence — Pre-Handover Implementation Scope

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Status:** SCOPE DOCUMENT ONLY — NOT AN IMPLEMENTATION AUTHORIZATION
**Origin:** GAP-001's expanded-scope domain sweep (Domain 7 & 30), registered as PHB-7, consolidated plan Tier 2 item 6
**Governs:** pre-handover parity blocker for the six per-module Executive Intelligence services (Sales, Production, Inventory, Shipping, Customer, Operations Risk) — Canon Domain 7 (Reporting & Business Intelligence, "Enterprise Intelligence" capability) and Domain 30 (Enterprise Analytics, Reporting Intelligence & Performance Management, "Trend intelligence / Forecasting / Performance Risk" capabilities)

This document is a precise implementation scope, built from a direct, complete read of all six frozen intelligence services (`ExecutiveSalesIntelligenceService.js`, `ExecutiveProductionIntelligenceService.js`, `ExecutiveInventoryIntelligenceService.js`, `ExecutiveShippingIntelligenceService.js`, `ExecutiveCustomerIntelligenceService.js`, `ExecutiveOperationsRiskService.js` — every one read in full, not excerpted), the Canon Domain 7 and Domain 30 Stage 10 certification texts, and a direct, re-verified read of Standalone's `reports-service.ts` internals (`readModules`, `primaryMetric`, `customerReader`, `readSource`). It is not a patch and contains no code. Writing or merging anything described here requires its own separate, explicit authorization and its own scope-lock/implementation/tests/regression/closure cycle. Standalone has not been handed over to employees; this is a pre-handover gap, not a live-incident fix.

## 0. What this scope targets, and a finding that tempers its own urgency

Canon Domain 7 names "Enterprise Intelligence" (cross-domain analytics, business measures, forecasts, scorecards, executive intelligence, §10.4) as a certified capability, and Domain 30 extends it with a full Performance Management governance layer: enterprise scorecards, performance targets/thresholds, variance management, performance reviews, trend intelligence, forecasting, scenario analysis, performance risk, and formal performance certification (§10.4). **None of that governance ceiling exists in frozen.** What frozen actually built is six independent, read-only analytics services — real, complete, internally consistent computation logic (trend lines, rankings, bottleneck/risk scoring, forecasts), but no Scorecard entity, no Target/Threshold entity, no Performance Review workflow, no Domain-30-style certification lifecycle. This document targets frozen's actual implemented computation logic, not Canon's fuller governance ceiling — consistent with every other scope document in this series.

**A finding that matters for how this gap should be read**: unlike PHB-5 (the Task Board, frozen's literal default screen) and PHB-6 (the Executive Dashboard, reachable from frozen's live router), **none of these six services are reachable through frozen's own live navigation**. Each has its own dedicated view (`View_ExecutiveSalesIntelligence.html`, `View_ExecutiveProductionIntelligence.html`, `View_ExecutiveInventoryIntelligence.html`, `View_ExecutiveShippingIntelligence.html`, `View_ExecutiveCustomerIntelligence.html`, `View_ExecutiveOperationsRisk.html`) that calls its service directly — confirmed by direct grep, these are not dead stubs, they're real wired UI — but `Router.js`'s `APP.MODULES` allowlist (`TodaysWork, Dashboard, CRM, Customers, Orders, Personalization, Production, Inventory, Procurement, Shipping, Finance`) does not include any of them, nor the `ExecutiveIntelligenceHub` that would otherwise link them together. This is the same orphaned-from-router situation already found for the Hub itself and for frozen's Assignment UI (Domain 25/8, this audit's expanded-scope sweep). Practically: **frozen employees cannot reach this functionality today either**, through the app's actual navigation. This does not make the gap non-existent — the computation logic is real and could be wired in (or ported into Standalone's own already-reachable Reports workspace) — but it changes the "employees would immediately miss this" framing that applies to PHB-5/PHB-6. Also confirmed directly: the Executive Dashboard's own "intelligence panel" (PHB-6, §2.4) does **not** call any of these six services — it independently (and, per PHB-6's own finding, largely emptily) tries to fuzzy-match a handful of trend/top-N field names out of the plain Orders/Finance workspace payload. These six services are currently disconnected from everything else in frozen, including the one related screen that is actually reachable.

## 1. Canon grounding

Domain 7 §10.4 names "Enterprise Intelligence" (cross-domain analytics, business measures, forecasts, scorecards, executive intelligence) as a Reporting-owned capability. Domain 30 §10.4 adds, as a distinct but related domain, "Trend intelligence. Forecasting. Scenario analysis. Performance Risk. Predictive performance intelligence. Executive Intelligence." Domain 30 §10.5 states its own relationship to Domain 7 explicitly: "The previously certified Reporting & Business Intelligence Domain remains authoritative for analytical delivery and reporting capabilities. This Domain extends that foundation into governed enterprise performance." Frozen does not implement this two-domain architectural split at all — the six Executive*Intelligence services sit alongside the Executive Dashboard as one loosely related ERP6.x family, with no formal boundary between "Reporting" and "Performance Management" content. This scope document does not propose introducing that split into Standalone either, since it would be new architecture beyond what frozen ever built.

## 2. What frozen actually implements — read in full, not excerpted

All six share a common shape: script-cache + lock-protected workspace builder (120-second-class TTL, exact values in each service's `Config.js`, not reproduced here), a `*GetWorkspace(forceRefresh)` entry point, an `*InvalidateCache()` entry point, and — for four of the six (Shipping, Customer, Operations Risk, and implicitly the others via the shared `executiveDashboardExecute_`/`executiveDashboardUnwrapResponse_` helpers from `ExecutiveDashboardResponse.js`) — a `{success, data}` / `{success:false, error}` response envelope. Each reads from one or more existing frozen read APIs (`ordersGetWorkspace`, `productionGetWorkspace`, `inventoryGetWorkspace`, `shippingLoadWorkspace`, `crmGetLeads`, etc. — the same underlying functions PHB-6's probing-adapter fallback tier already uses) via its own normalization layer, and none of the six mutate anything — confirmed by direct read, no write calls anywhere in any of the six files.

### 2.1 Sales Intelligence (`ExecutiveSalesIntelligenceService.js`, 121 lines)

Reads Orders + Finance. Computes: a `summary`/`comparisons` block (today/yesterday/MTD sales and orders, average order value with a fallback calculation — `today/ordersToday` when Orders doesn't supply an AOV directly — and day-over-day / month-over-month growth percentages, `:83-95`); `salesTrend` (finance-preferred, orders-fallback, capped at `MAX_TREND_POINTS`, `:97`); `channelPerformance` sorted by sales descending (`:98`); `topProducts`/`topCustomers` sorted by sales, capped at `MAX_TOP_ROWS` (`:99-100`); `orderAging` — open orders bucketed by hours-since-creation into frozen-defined buckets (`:102-107`); `delayedOrderRisks` — open orders scored Low/Medium/High/Critical by age and overdue-vs-promised-date, filtered to High/Critical only, capped at `MAX_RISK_ROWS` (`:109`, risk logic at `:119`); a `forecast` — simple trailing-7-day average projected forward one day and seven days (`:111`).

### 2.2 Production Intelligence (`ExecutiveProductionIntelligenceService.js`, 299 lines)

Reads Production + Orders (merging order commitments — due date, customer, priority — onto jobs that lack them, `:72-88`). Computes: a `summary` (queued/in-progress/completed-today/blocked/overdue job counts, target-achievement percent, average cycle time — computed from actual start/complete timestamps when the source doesn't already supply it, `:90-120`); `throughputTrend` (completed vs. target per day, `:122-132`); `workstationPerformance` — derived per-station load merged with any station-level fields the source supplies (utilization/efficiency/downtime), sorted by load, capped at `MAX_WORKSTATIONS` (`:134-158`); `bottlenecks` — a composite score (`load×10`, `+20` if utilization >90%, `+20` if efficiency <70%, `+≤20` for downtime minutes), filtered to non-Low severity, capped at `MAX_BOTTLENECKS` (`:160-180`); `jobAging` (non-completed jobs bucketed by age, tracking blocked/overdue counts per bucket, `:182-201`); `deliveryRisks` — per-job risk (Low/Medium/High/Critical) from status/age/overdue-hours/hours-until-due/priority, filtered to High/Critical, capped at `MAX_RISK_ROWS` (`:203-230`, risk logic at `:269-276`); `stageDistribution` (`:232-242`); a `forecast` — trailing-7-day average completed jobs, plus estimated days to clear the current active-job backlog at that rate (`:244-255`).

### 2.3 Inventory Intelligence (`ExecutiveInventoryIntelligenceService.js`, 126 lines)

Reads Inventory + Production (material demand from production requirements) + Procurement (suppliers). Enriches each item with available quantity (on-hand minus reserved), production demand, days-of-cover, inventory value, days-since-last-movement, and a derived stock status (Out of stock / Critical / Low / Overstock / Healthy — thresholds in `Config.js`, `:48-68`, status logic `:119`). Computes: `summary` (counts per status, dead/slow-moving counts, total inventory value with a derived fallback if the source doesn't supply one, stock-health percent, `:69-86`); `stockHealth` — items sorted by status severity, capped at `MAX_STOCK_ROWS` (`:87-90`); `criticalShortages` — items where production demand exceeds availability, with affected-job counts, sorted by shortage size (`:91-95`); `inventoryValueByCategory` (`:96-99`); `movementClassification` — fast/active/slow/dead/unknown buckets by days-since-movement (`:100-103`); `supplierDependency` — per-supplier item count, inventory value, critical-item count, and a composite dependency score, capped at `MAX_SUPPLIERS` (`:104-108`); `replenishmentPlan` — items needing reorder, with a recommended order quantity derived from daily usage, lead time, and a default reorder-window constant, capped and prioritized (`:109-114`); `stockCoverage` — items bucketed by days-of-cover (`:115-118`).

### 2.4 Shipping Intelligence (`ExecutiveShippingIntelligenceService.js`, 506 lines)

Reads Shipping + Orders + Finance. Computes: `dispatchReadiness` — orders not yet shipped, split into ready-for-dispatch / awaiting-packing / awaiting-production based on packing/production status fields, with counts (`:106-158`); `slaRisks` — orders past or approaching a promised-dispatch deadline (falling back to a default SLA-hours-from-creation when no explicit promise date exists), severity-scored, sorted by hours-remaining ascending (`:160-209`); `courierPerformance` — per-courier shipment counts, delivery-success/RTO/NDR rates, average shipping cost, average delivery hours (`:211-270`); `channelPerformance` — same shape, per sales channel, plus shipping-cost-as-percent-of-revenue (`:272-321`); `deliveryExceptions` — separate NDR and RTO lists with reason/destination/order-value (`:323-355`); `shippingCosts` — total/average cost, percent of revenue, per-courier breakdown, preferring a Finance-supplied total over the derived sum when available (`:357-393`); `dispatchForecast` — a 7-day trailing daily-dispatch trend plus a next-day projection (`:395-420`); a `summary` rolling up totals, delivery-success rate, RTO exposure value, and cost figures (`:423-456`).

### 2.5 Customer Intelligence (`ExecutiveCustomerIntelligenceService.js`, 513 lines)

Reads CRM leads + Customers + Orders. Computes: `pipeline` — lead counts/value by stage, conversion rate (`:121-162`); `leadSourcePerformance` — per-source lead counts, converted counts, conversion rate, sorted by converted count (`:164-203`); `followUpDiscipline` — every open lead classified Overdue/Due Soon/Missing/Scheduled by follow-up date, with counts and a sorted row list (`:205-275`); `customerValue` — per-customer order count, revenue, average order value, first/last order date, days-since-last-order, repeat-customer flag, dormancy flag (against a `DORMANT_DAYS` threshold), rolled up into total/purchasing/repeat customer counts, repeat-purchase rate, and customer lifetime value as the mean revenue of purchasing customers (`:277-384`); `concentrationRisk` — customers ranked by revenue share of total, flagged High risk above a concentration-percent threshold (`:386-420`); `reactivation` — dormant customers with positive historical revenue, prioritized High/Medium/Standard by revenue size (`:422-448`); a `summary` rolling up all of the above into one object (`:450-472`).

### 2.6 Operations Risk (`ExecutiveOperationsRiskService.js`, 368 lines)

**Reads its own separate normalization of the raw Production/Inventory/Shipping/Finance/Customer source data — it does not call the other five Intelligence services or consume their outputs.** This is a distinct, sixth independent read of the underlying modules, not a rollup of Sales/Production/Inventory/Shipping/Customer Intelligence — worth being precise about, since the name suggests a dependency that doesn't actually exist in frozen's code. Computes five risk-generator functions, each producing raw risk entries: Production (capacity-percent pressure above a warning threshold, and SLA/overdue risk against due-time, `:98-141`); Inventory (below-reorder-level and low-days-of-cover risk, `:143-178`); Shipping (SLA risk against promised delivery time for undelivered shipments, `:180-207`); Finance (overdue receivables, scored by days overdue, `:209-232`); Customer (dormant high-value customers, `:234-251`). Every risk is finalized with a clamped 0-100 score and a Low/Medium/Critical severity band (`:253-275`), then the whole set is sorted by score and rolled up into a `summary` (total/critical/medium counts, total financial exposure, total customer exposure, highest score, `:277-294`), `criticalRisks` (filtered list), `moduleExposure` (per-module risk/critical/financial/customer rollup, `:296-324`), `financialExposure` and `customerExposure` (risks with nonzero impact, sorted, `:326-340`).

## 3. What already exists in Standalone

`apps/api/src/reports-service.ts`'s `readModules()` (lines 1377-1403) wires eight reader slots — `dashboard, sales (→orders), production, inventory, shipping, finance, customer, operationsRisk`. Six of the eight are raw pass-throughs of each module's existing `loadWorkspace()`-style output (no computation); `customer` is a real, distinct reader (`customerReader`, lines 1353-1372) but, directly re-checked, it only bundles raw `{crm, customers}` data with no pipeline/conversion/follow-up/CLV computation whatsoever — plumbing exists, computation doesn't. `operationsRisk` is hard-coded to `undefined` (line 1391, re-confirmed directly again in this pass) — that reader slot can never connect to any data, regardless of what's built for the other modules. **The one exception, already complete**: `executiveFinanceIntelligence()` (lines 1732-1838, reachable via `GET /reports/financial`) is a full, apparently faithful port of frozen's Finance Intelligence logic — this is not part of this scope's build target, since it already exists.

**A precise, load-bearing implementation detail**: Standalone's `primaryMetric()` (lines 1405-1461) does not read frozen's field vocabulary. It reads its own, already-fixed vocabulary off each module's `.summary` object: `sales` expects `summary.revenue`/`summary.totalOrders`; `production` expects `summary.activeJobs`/`summary.overdueJobs`; `inventory` expects `summary.stockAlerts`/`summary.lowStockItems`; `shipping` expects `summary.openShipments`/`summary.slaRisk`; `finance` expects `summary.grossProfit`/`summary.overdueReceivables`; `customer` expects `summary.totalCustomers`/`summary.pipelineValue`; `operationsRisk` expects `summary.criticalRisks`/`summary.totalFinancialExposure`. **Whoever implements this scope needs to populate Standalone's own field names, not frozen's** — frozen's `summary` shapes (§2.1-§2.6 above) are the computation reference, not the exact output contract to replicate field-for-field.

## 4. Proposed Standalone scope

Six independent per-module builds (Operations Risk does not depend on the other five being built first, per §2.6's finding), each following the same shape: a new application-service method reading from the existing `loadWorkspace()` sources already available (`order-service.ts`, `production-service.ts`, `inventory-service.ts`, `shipping-service.ts`, `crm-service.ts`, `customer-service.ts`, plus the existing `procurement` reader for supplier data), computing the fields enumerated in §2, and populating both a rich workspace object (for a future dedicated view, mirroring frozen's per-module `View_Executive*.html`) and the specific `summary.*` field names `primaryMetric()` already expects (§3), so the existing Reports workspace picks it up automatically without further changes to `reports-service.ts` itself beyond replacing each raw-pass-through reader with the new computed one.

- **4.1 Sales Intelligence** — reads Orders + Finance workspaces; computes §2.1's summary/comparisons/trend/channel/top-N/aging/risk/forecast; populates `summary.revenue`/`summary.totalOrders`.
- **4.2 Production Intelligence** — reads Production + Orders workspaces; computes §2.2's summary/throughput/workstation/bottleneck/aging/risk/stage/forecast; populates `summary.activeJobs`/`summary.overdueJobs`.
- **4.3 Inventory Intelligence** — reads Inventory + Production + Procurement workspaces; computes §2.3's summary/health/shortages/value/movement/supplier/replenishment/coverage; populates `summary.stockAlerts`/`summary.lowStockItems`.
- **4.4 Shipping Intelligence** — reads Shipping + Orders + Finance workspaces; computes §2.4's readiness/SLA/courier/channel/exceptions/costs/forecast/summary; populates `summary.openShipments`/`summary.slaRisk`.
- **4.5 Customer Intelligence** — reads CRM + Customers + Orders workspaces (the `customerReader` plumbing already exists, per §3 — this is the lowest-effort starting point of the six, since the reader wiring is the only piece already done); computes §2.5's pipeline/source/follow-up/value/concentration/reactivation/summary; populates `summary.totalCustomers`/`summary.pipelineValue`.
- **4.6 Operations Risk** — reads Production + Inventory + Shipping + Finance + Customer workspaces directly (not the other five Intelligence outputs, per §2.6); computes §2.6's five risk generators, finalization, and rollups; populates `summary.criticalRisks`/`summary.totalFinancialExposure`; this is also the piece PHB-6's Dashboard scope document flagged as "structurally disconnected" (the hard-coded `undefined` reader) — fixing this reader is the direct, minimal action needed to connect it, independent of whether the fuller computation in this section is also built.

**Threshold values** (aging-bucket boundaries, risk-score cutoffs, dormancy/slow-moving/dead-stock day counts, SLA warning hours, concentration-risk percent, capacity-warning percent, default lead-time/reorder-window days) are defined in each frozen service's own `Config.js` file and were not individually enumerated in this pass — read the relevant `Config.js` at implementation time for exact frozen values if literal parity on thresholds is wanted, or set Standalone-native values deliberately, which this document does not decide.

## 5. Cross-cutting notes

**Relationship to PHB-6.** The Executive Dashboard's intelligence panel (`salesTrend`/`topProducts`/`topCustomers`/`channelPerformance`/`manufacturingPerformance`) and this document's §4.1/§4.2 cover overlapping ground. Per PHB-6's own scope document (§5), whichever is built first should feed the other rather than duplicating the computation — this document does not re-decide that sequencing question, only confirms the overlap is real on inspection of both sides' source.

**These six services were real and tested in frozen, but not reachable by employees there either (§0).** This is worth keeping in view when this item is prioritized against PHB-5/PHB-6: it is a smaller loss to Standalone's day-one usability than either of those two, in the specific sense that frozen's own employees aren't using this today through the app's actual navigation — though the underlying computation is still real, valuable capability that Canon names and that a fuller Standalone product would eventually want.

**Customer Intelligence is the lowest-effort starting point**, not because the computation is simpler (it isn't, particularly — follow-up discipline and concentration risk are as involved as anything in Sales or Production) but because `reports-service.ts` already has a dedicated `customerReader` wired to real CRM/Customer data sources, rather than a raw pass-through that would need to be replaced — one less piece of plumbing to build.

**Operations Risk's disconnection is a one-line-of-consequence bug independent of the fuller build.** `reports-service.ts:1391`'s hard-coded `undefined` means this module can never connect *at all*, even to placeholder or partial data, regardless of whether §4.6's full computation is authorized. Whoever picks up any part of this scope should know that fixing that one reader is a prerequisite for Operations Risk to work at all, not an optional nicety.

## 8. Implementation record, 2026-09-15 — §4.5 Customer Intelligence and §4.6 Operations Risk

Authorized and implemented in this session, following §7's steps (1) and (2) exactly — the two lowest-effort, highest-leverage pieces (Operations Risk's reader was a genuine one-line unblock; Customer Intelligence's reader plumbing already existed). **§4.1–§4.4 (Sales, Production, Inventory, Shipping Intelligence) were not built in this increment** — each is comparably sized to what was built here and is better treated as its own separate increment than rushed alongside this one; see the note at the end of this record. **Nothing has been committed or pushed.**

**Built — Customer Intelligence (§4.5):** pure computation functions in `packages/domain/src/executive-customer-intelligence.ts` — `computeExecutiveCustomerPipeline`, `computeExecutiveCustomerSourcePerformance`, `computeExecutiveCustomerFollowUpDiscipline`, `computeExecutiveCustomerValue`, `computeExecutiveCustomerConcentrationRisk`, `computeExecutiveCustomerReactivation`, `buildExecutiveCustomerIntelligenceSummary` — each a direct, field-by-field port of frozen's own functions, read in full from `ExecutiveCustomerIntelligenceService.js`/`ExecutiveCustomerIntelligenceNormalizers.js`/`Config.js` before writing any of this, not reconstructed from the scope summary above. Threshold defaults match frozen exactly: `DORMANT_DAYS: 90`, `FOLLOW_UP_WARNING_DAYS: 3`, `HIGH_CONCENTRATION_PERCENT: 25`. An application service (`apps/api/src/executive-customer-intelligence-service.ts`) composing three already-existing reads — `crm.getLeads()`, `customers.listCustomers()`, `orders.listOrders()` — no new persistence.

**Built — Operations Risk (§4.6):** pure computation functions in `packages/domain/src/executive-operations-risk.ts` — one risk generator per source module (`computeExecutiveProductionRisks`, `computeExecutiveInventoryRisks`, `computeExecutiveShippingRisks`, `computeExecutiveFinanceRisks`, `computeExecutiveCustomerRisks`), `finalizeExecutiveOperationsRisk` (score clamp + severity band), and the summary/exposure rollups (`buildExecutiveOperationsRiskSummary`, `...ModuleExposure`, `...FinancialExposure`, `...CustomerExposure`) — also read from frozen's `ExecutiveOperationsRiskService.js`/`Normalizers.js`/`Config.js` in full before writing. Threshold defaults match frozen exactly: `HIGH_RISK_SCORE: 70`, `MEDIUM_RISK_SCORE: 40`, `SLA_WARNING_HOURS: 24`. An application service (`apps/api/src/executive-operations-risk-service.ts`) composing five sources — Production, Inventory, Shipping, Finance, and (deliberately) the Customer Intelligence service just built above, not an independent customer read — each independently try/caught, matching frozen's own per-provider connection tracking and the graceful-degradation pattern already established for the Today's Work aggregator and the Executive Dashboard.

**§7's flagged one-line bug, fixed:** `reports-service.ts:1391`'s hard-coded `operationsRisk: undefined` now reads `dependencies.operationsRisk`, wired in `server.ts` to the new Operations Risk service. `customerReader(dependencies)`'s raw `{crm, customers}` pass-through is now only a fallback (`dependencies.customerIntelligence ?? customerReader(dependencies)`) behind the new computed Customer Intelligence service, preserving backward compatibility for any caller that doesn't wire the new dependency. **`primaryMetric()` itself needed no changes** — confirmed directly, both new workspaces already populate the exact `summary.*` field names it expects for the `customer`/`operationsRisk` keys (§3's own finding), by design.

**Real data gaps found and handled honestly, not papered over — a repeat of the PHB-6 `efficiency: null` pattern, applied here to two of frozen's six risk sub-generators:**
- Frozen's Production **capacity-pressure** risk needs a `capacityPercent` field; Standalone's `ProductionJob` has no equivalent at all. Not built — fabricating a percentage would be worse than omitting it.
- Frozen's Inventory **low-days-of-cover** risk needs a daily-usage-rate figure only the fuller Inventory Intelligence build (§4.3, not this increment) would supply; the *other* Inventory risk generator (below-reorder-level) needs no such figure and was built.
- Financial-impact figures frozen sources from a job/shipment "value" field that doesn't exist on Standalone's `ProductionJob` or `Shipment` either (and likely wouldn't reliably exist on frozen's own *raw, unmerged* workspace read for Operations Risk specifically either — Production Intelligence's separate order-merge step, not used here, is what would normally supply it in frozen). Production risk financial impact is `0`, documented as a deliberate choice; Shipping risk financial impact uses `codAmount` (the closest real "value at risk" figure on a `Shipment`) when nonzero, `0` otherwise.

**A design choice worth calling out explicitly: Operations Risk's Customer generator consumes Customer Intelligence's own computed output, not an independent read.** §2.6 of this document characterized Operations Risk as reading "its own separate normalization... it does not call the other five Intelligence services." Rereading frozen's actual `ExecutiveOperationsRiskConfig.js` while implementing this found a nuance worth recording: its `CUSTOMER` provider candidate list tries `executiveCustomerIntelligenceGetWorkspace` *before* falling back to a raw customer read — meaning frozen itself, when Customer Intelligence happens to be deployed, prefers its computed dormancy flag over an independent derivation. Since this increment builds both services together, having Operations Risk consume Customer Intelligence's real output (rather than re-deriving dormancy independently) is *more* faithful to frozen's actual preference order, not a deviation from it — and avoids computing the same dormancy logic twice.

**Verification:** 21 new pure-function tests (10 for Customer Intelligence, 11 for Operations Risk, each hand-verified against frozen's exact arithmetic before running — all passed on the first run), 7 application-service tests (composition, graceful degradation on a thrown source, the Customer-Intelligence-feeds-Operations-Risk composition), 4 wiring tests proving the `reports-service.ts` reader-slot fix and fallback behavior. Full monorepo build clean. Full regression suite re-run: domain 281/281, platform 231/231, api 643/644 — the one failure is the same pre-existing, unrelated `reports-service.ts` timezone issue tracked since PHB-1.

**Not built in this increment (§4.1–§4.4, deliberately deferred):** Sales, Production, Inventory, and Shipping Intelligence. Each is comparably sized to what was built here (Shipping Intelligence's frozen source alone is 506 lines; Customer Intelligence's is 513) — building all six per-module services in one pass risked a rushed, under-verified result, which this session has consistently avoided elsewhere (see PHB-5's task-store/aggregator split, PHB-6's Phase A/Phase B split). §7 itself takes no position on ordering among these four, since none depends on another; whoever continues this item can pick any order.

## 9. Implementation record, 2026-09-16 — §4.1 Sales Intelligence

Authorized and implemented in this session, continuing directly from §8, picking Sales Intelligence (§4.1) as the next of the four remaining modules — §7 itself is neutral on ordering among them, so this was a pragmatic pick, not a signal the other three are lower priority. **Production, Inventory, and Shipping Intelligence (§4.2–§4.4) remain unbuilt.** Read `ExecutiveSalesIntelligenceService.js`/`ExecutiveSalesIntelligenceConfig.js` in full before writing any of this, not reconstructed from the summary above. **Nothing has been committed or pushed.**

**Built:** pure computation functions in `packages/domain/src/executive-sales-intelligence.ts` — `computeExecutiveSalesGrowthPercent`, `computeExecutiveSalesPeriodTotals`, `computeExecutiveSalesMetrics` (summary + comparisons), `computeExecutiveSalesTrend`, `computeExecutiveSalesChannelPerformance`, `computeExecutiveSalesTopCustomers`, `computeExecutiveSalesOrderAging`, `computeExecutiveSalesRiskLevel`/`computeExecutiveSalesDelayedOrderRisks`, `computeExecutiveSalesForecast` — each a field-by-field port of frozen's own functions. Threshold defaults match frozen exactly: `MAX_TREND_POINTS: 31`, `MAX_TOP_ROWS: 10`, `MAX_RISK_ROWS: 15`, and the four `ORDER_AGING_BUCKETS` (same day / 1–2 days / 3–5 days / 6+ days).
